3Patient growth strategy

The goal is booked appointments, not likes. Every channel points to one action: a WhatsApp message to 0333-2156512. Each enquiry is then tracked until the patient visits.

1. Local Google presence

  • Verify the Google Business Profile so the clinic shows for "physiotherapist near me" in Malir.
  • Add services, real photos, logo and cover once verified.
  • Ask satisfied patients for genuine Google reviews (never written by us).

2. Consistent content, 4–5 posts a week

  • Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri, Sat at 8:30 PM PKT.
  • Mostly short educational reels in Roman Urdu and Urdu, faceless where possible.
  • Every post ends with WhatsApp number, location and hours.

3. Click-to-WhatsApp ads

  • Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) that open a WhatsApp chat.
  • Radius of 5–8 km around the hospital.
  • Start with the best-performing reels; budget to be set by Waqar.
  • Judge by cost per enquiry and per booked patient.

4. WhatsApp enquiry tracking

  • Greeting, away message and quick replies so no enquiry waits.
  • Reply within 1 hour during clinic hours.
  • Log every enquiry: New → Contacted → Booked → Visited → Follow-up → Closed.
  • Collect only name, contact and the problem; no medical records in the tracker.

5. Referrals

  • Build links with doctors inside Al Mumtaz Hospital (orthopaedic, neurology, paediatrics).
  • Nearby GPs, gyms and sports clubs.
  • Record "How did you hear about us?" for every new patient.

6. Monthly review

  • Each Monday, fill the weekly results in the calendar's tracker sheet.
  • Monthly: enquiries, bookings, visits, ad spend, cost per patient.
  • Keep topics and ads that bring patients; drop the rest.
Compliance: no promises such as "cure" or "guaranteed relief", no patient faces without written consent, and every medical tip is approved by Dr. Arshad before it is posted.

5Faceless reels, batch 1

Four vertical videos (1080×1920, about 28.5 seconds, copyright-free background music) are rendered and saved in videos/. Each follows the same structure: hook, short explanation, 3 tips, when to see a physio, and an end card with logo, WhatsApp, location and hours, plus "General awareness only. Not a diagnosis."

01 · Neck pain from mobile usePost 2 · Tue 24 Nov
Mobile dekhte dekhte GARDAN mein dard?
  1. Hold your phone at eye level
  2. Take a short break every 20–30 minutes
  3. Relax your shoulders and sit upright
See a physio if pain persists or there is numbness in the hands.
02 · Back pain from long sittingPost 3 · Thu 26 Nov
Ghanton baith kar KAMAR mein dard?
  1. Keep your lower back supported
  2. Stand up and walk every 30 minutes
  3. Bend your knees when lifting
See a physio if pain spreads to the legs or persists.
04 · Frozen shoulderPost 4 · Fri 27 Nov
Baazu upar uthane mein MUSHKIL? Kandhay mein akran?
  1. Don't ignore ongoing shoulder pain
  2. Don't force painful movements
  3. Learn guided exercises from a physiotherapist
See a physio if pain worsens at night or the arm feels weak.
03 · Knee pain on stairsPost 5 · Sat 28 Nov
Seerhiyan chadhte waqt GHUTNON mein dard?
  1. A healthy weight means less load on your knees
  2. Gentle daily walking keeps joints moving
  3. Learn the right exercises from a physiotherapist
See a physio if there is swelling or difficulty walking.

Dr. Arshad's review points for the reels

  • Every tip and "when to see a physio" line is accurate and safe as general advice.
  • The "20–30 minutes" (neck) and "30 minutes" (back) break intervals are acceptable.
  • The frozen shoulder wording ("can be a sign") is suitably cautious.
  • The Urdu spelling on screen is correct.
